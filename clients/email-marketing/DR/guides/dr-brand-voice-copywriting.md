# Digital Resource: Brand Voice & Copywriting Style Guide

Reference for Claude Code and anyone else writing copy for Digital Resource (DR). It is based on:

1. the copy in all 18 Cooling Drip emails in Canva (2024 originals and 2025–26 rewrites),
2. editorial feedback left by DR's marketing leads on 2022–2025 Above the Scroll newsletter drafts in Basecamp,
3. the DR brand guide (tagline *"Your resource for everything digital"*, #WeAreDR), and
4. the review of all 160 Above the Scroll to-dos and reports (Jan 2019–Sept 2025) that shaped the 2027 plan (section 10).

Where the newer rewrites differ from the 2024 originals, this guide follows the newer ones. The team has been moving away from hype and toward plain, empathetic copy, and that is the direction to keep going.

---

## 1. Who we're talking to

- **The reader is a small or mid-sized business owner or decision maker**, not a marketer. They're busy, know their own business deeply, and know digital marketing loosely. Many are clients, partners or warm leads.
- Write so the owner understands the value without knowing the jargon. One editor's note: *"bring the marketing terminology down a little bit to meet our audience in the middle."*
- Choose topics a business owner cares about: customers, leads, visibility, sales, time and money. Skip topics that belong to a social-media manager's job, such as influencer tactics or platform minutiae.
- Never call readers "subscribers". They are clients, partners and peers.

## 2. Voice in one line

**A sharp, friendly marketing partner who tells you what's really going on and what to do next, without the hype.**

### Voice pillars

| Pillar | What it means | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| **Helpful expert** | Explain why something matters and give 3–4 concrete moves | Lecturing, or listing services |
| **Plain-spoken** | Short sentences, everyday words, one idea per paragraph | Buzzwords, stacked adjectives, marketing-speak |
| **Empathetic** | Start from the owner's problem ("Navigating the digital maze can be overwhelming, but don't sweat it!") | Fear-mongering or guilt |
| **Confident, with proof** | Real client results, numbers and names | Unbacked superlatives ("revolutionize", "dominate") |
| **Warm with a wink** | A light aside now and then ("Smart, isn't it?", "Yep, you read that right.") | Pun overload. Editors flagged emails as "a little too punny for our brand voice." |
| **People-first** | DR is a team ("The Digital Resource Team", BTS stories, #WeAreDR) | A faceless corporation |

### Tone dial by context

| Context | Formal ↔ casual | Playful | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cooling Drip / nurture | Mid | Low–mid | Teach first, then make one ask |
| Above the Scroll newsletter | Mid–casual | Mid | Timely topics and team culture are welcome; holiday themes are fine with a light touch |
| Client-facing reports / service pages | Mid–formal | Low | Clear and results-led |
| Social captions / BTS | Casual | Mid–high | Personal: what makes this team member unique |

## 3. Patterns that work (keep doing these)

**Headlines.** Ten words or fewer, using one of these formulas:

- **Pain question:** "Are You Paying to Lose Customers?" / "Not Sure Where You're Losing People?"
- **Stat hook:** "46% of all searches on Google are for a local business or local service." / "It takes 7–8 touchpoints to turn a lead into a sale."
- **Reframe:** "Stop Losing Customers You Already Have" / "Customers Need to See It to Believe It"
- **Promise:** "What If You Could Get 55% More Website Traffic with One Simple Strategy?"

**Openers that reframe the problem.** Strongest example:
> "The good news? You don't need more leads. You need better conversations with the ones you already have."

**Question → answer rhythm.** "Can your social posts influence your SEO? Absolutely!" / "Need proof?" Use it at most once per section.

**Everyday analogies.** "Just like your favorite restaurant's menu keeps you returning, your site needs to serve fresh, tasty content regularly." / "Think of SEO as the engine in your car." Use one per email at most.

**Proof with names and numbers.** "See how Happy Hemp… boost[ed] their market presence by 60%." Show results rather than describe them. Editor's note: *"What was the result of the custom website? Did it drive more customers?"*

**Tips as subhead + 2–3 lines.** For example, "Set Clear Objectives:" followed by what to do and why.

**Themed sign-offs.** "Cheers to your journey," / "Your partner in digital growth," / "Let's Turn Your Leads into Loyal Fans," followed by **The Digital Resource Team**. Match the sign-off to the email's topic.

## 4. Patterns to retire (seen in older copy)

| Avoid | Why | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Hype verbs: *supercharge, revolutionize, dominate, skyrocket, unlock the full potential, elevate, transform* | They sound like every agency, and editors called "cutting-edge" "a little lazy" | Say the concrete outcome: "bring more people back to your site" |
| Hype nouns: *secret weapon, game-changer, magic, powerhouse* | Overused and vague | Name the actual benefit |
| *Seamlessly, leverage, synergy, robust, holistic* | Jargon | Use plain verbs: use, connect, combine |
| "Imagine…" openers stacked back to back | They read as a sales pitch | Open with the reader's real situation |
| Topics or wording aimed at marketers | Wrong audience | Translate to owner outcomes |
| Repeated lead-ins ("Learn more about…" three times in a row) | Monotonous; editors flagged it | Vary them, or cut to the verb |
| Redundancies ("blog article") | Editors flagged it | "blog" or "article", not both |
| Puns in every section | Off-voice | One light theme touch per email |

## 5. Calls to action

- **One primary CTA per email**, repeated at most twice (in the hero and in the closing band). The audit found emails with 4–6 CTAs, which dilutes the click.
- Start with a verb and say what the reader gets: "Book Your Free Consultation", "Get a Free Strategy Review", "Download the SEO Checklist", "Read the Happy Hemp Story".
- Add urgency or ease when it's honest ("Now", "Free", "30-Minute").
- Button colour follows intent, per the brand guide: green for consult or contact, orange for downloads and media, blue for "learn more" and secondary links.
- Never use "Click Here" or "Submit".

## 6. Mechanics (house style sheet)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Person | Second person ("you, your business"). DR is "we". |
| Contractions | Yes (you're, it's, don't) |
| Sentence length | Average under 20 words. Paragraphs of 1–3 sentences. |
| Headlines | Title Case. No end punctuation except "?" |
| Buttons | ALL CAPS in the design, but write them in Title Case in copy docs. Never mix cases in one label (the audit found "Learn more ABOUT SOCIAL MEDIA"). |
| Tip subheads | Title Case with a trailing colon ("Set Clear Objectives:"). Be consistent within an email. Don't number some and not others. |
| Em dashes | Use sparingly, for real emphasis. Editor rule: *"Reserve em dashes for when you want to make an impact with what comes after."* Never use one where a comma works. |
| Comma splices | No. "Here's what most businesses don't realize, it's not…" should use a colon or a dash. |
| Numbers | Numerals for stats (46%, 7–8). Use thousands separators ("84,561 views", not "84561"). |
| Stats | **Every stat needs a source** in the copy doc (Google, HubSpot, Wyzowl, client data). Put the date on any stat older than 2 years, or refresh it. |
| Emoji | OK in subject lines (one at most). Rarely in body text. Never in headlines inside the design. |
| Client names | Use the client's own brand language (for example "practitioner-assisted stretch studio" for Stretch Zone, "BioHorizons" spelled out). Get approval before naming results. |
| Copyright / dates | Current year. Check every send. |
| Industry-specific content | Only send it to the matching segment (for example the healthcare "Internal Practice Marketing" block). |

## 7. Writing process (for Claude Code or a writer)

1. **Brief.** Note the audience segment, the single goal, the one CTA, a proof asset (client story or stat with a source), and the email's position in the series.
2. **Pick a headline formula** (section 3). Write 3 options and keep the most specific one.
3. **Draft to the structure:** hook → why it matters (2 short paragraphs) → 3–4 tips (subhead plus 2–3 lines each) → proof → CTA band → themed sign-off.
4. **Run the voice pass:**
   - remove every word from the section-4 table
   - cap em dashes at 2 per email
   - make sure each tip describes an owner outcome
5. **Trim.** Descriptions should be 1–2 lines (editor note: *"Short and sweet does the trick!"*). The email should be readable in about 60 seconds.
6. **Write the subject line and preheader** (2 options for A/B):
   - Subject: 6–9 words, curiosity or benefit, at most 1 emoji. The preheader should add a second hook, not repeat the subject.
   - Topical and trending subjects lift opens, but direct, benefit-led subjects lift clicks. **Clicks are DR's main number**, so default to benefit-led subjects and put any trending hook in the preheader.
   - Judge A/B tests on click rate, not open rate.
7. **QA:**
   - one CTA
   - stats sourced
   - no comma splices or mixed-case buttons
   - client names correct
   - current year
   - read it aloud once
   - every link has UTMs (`utm_campaign=<series>-<yyyy>-<mm>`)

## 8. Before → after examples

**Hype → plain**
> Before: "Imagine leveraging powerful SEO combined with strategic content marketing to significantly boost your search rankings."
> After: "Pair SEO with regular, useful content, and more of the right people find you on Google and stick around."

**Telling → showing**
> Before: "Custom content can ensure a consistent brand identity across platforms, making your brand unforgettable."
> After: "When every post looks and sounds like you, people recognize you faster. Happy Hemp saw engagement double."

**Splice → clean**
> Before: "But here's what most businesses don't realize, it's not just about being found once."
> After: "But here's what most businesses miss: it's not about being found once."

## 9. Prompt snippet for Claude Code

```
Write as Digital Resource (DR): a sharp, friendly marketing partner for small and
mid-sized business owners. Plain words, short sentences, second person, contractions.
Lead with the owner's problem, give 3–4 concrete tips (subhead + 2–3 lines), show
proof with a named client or sourced stat, end with ONE verb-first CTA and a themed
sign-off + "The Digital Resource Team". Avoid hype words (supercharge, revolutionize,
dominate, secret weapon, game-changer, seamlessly, leverage, cutting-edge). Max 2 em
dashes, no comma splices, Title Case headlines, consistent button casing, at most one
light pun. Follow dr-brand-voice-copywriting.md for full rules.
```

## 10. Above the Scroll (monthly newsletter), from 2027

Lessons from 2019–2025 sends, turned into rules. The full plan is in `../copy/2027-above-the-scroll-topics-plan.md`.

**What readers responded to:** SEO, Google and AI topics (most-clicked every time), one-click polls (far more responses than quizzes), testimonials and behind-the-scenes content (the only DR content clicked reliably).
**What didn't work:** trending subject lines (more opens, fewer clicks), long issues with many links, quizzes on a separate app, a "free analysis" section that changed every month.

**Build every issue from these fields first, in this order.** The body is written around them, never the other way round:

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| Month / Theme | A light theme touch (holiday, season). One per issue, no pun in every section. |
| Topic | One owner problem. At least 7 of 12 months lead with search, Google or AI. |
| Purpose | One sentence: what this issue does for the reader and for DR |
| H1 | 10 words or fewer, a section-3 formula. The intro answers it. |
| Subject line | Benefit-led, 6–9 words, at most 1 emoji |
| Pretext (preheader) | A second hook. Trending angles go here. |
| CTA | One, verb-first, written before the body. Button colour by intent. |

**Issue structure (about 230 words, a 60-second read):** H1 → bold lead sentence + 2 short paragraphs → "The 60-Second Version" (3 tips, subhead with colon + 2 lines) → one client proof line with a number → Quick Poll (one question, 3–4 one-click answers) → Behind the Screens (one line about a real team member) → CTA band → themed sign-off + The Digital Resource Team.

**Poll copy:** question in Title Case ending in "?"; answers 3–6 words in Title Case, written as outcomes ("Getting More Good Reviews"), not services ("Reputation Management"). Always say when results come back ("We'll share the results in February.").

**Behind the Screens copy:** name, role and one human detail. Invite a reply ("Hit reply and your note lands in [Name]'s inbox."). Replies help deliverability and show people read it.

**Re-engagement tone (after a gap):** say it plainly, once ("It's been a while, and we're glad you're still here."). No apology paragraph, no "we've been busy" excuses. Get to the useful part in the next sentence.

**Franchise version:** same issue. Swap one tip, the proof and, if needed, the subject to speak to multi-location owners ("every location", "the whole brand").

**Stats in newsletters:** prefer DR client numbers (approved, dated) over industry stats. If you use an industry stat, source and date it in the copy doc.
